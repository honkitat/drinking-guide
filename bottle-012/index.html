BACKGROUND

Moutai is not a brand name that can be borrowed: only baijiu made in the town of Maotai, on the Chishui river in Guizhou, may carry it. The town's humidity, its water and its local qu (the fermentation starter) are treated as the reason the spirit tastes the way it does, and the 「飞天」 Flying Fairy label is the export-facing one — the same liquid as the domestic star label, different politics.

The cycle is slow by any standard: the qu is made in summer, the sorghum is fermented and distilled over the following year, and the spirit is then blended and rested — at least five years from start to bottle. 53% vol is not an accident either; it is the strength at which the aroma compounds stay in solution, which is why Moutai is drunk in tiny cups rather than poured over ice.

The back of the box is unusually plain-spoken for a luxury brand: 酱香型白酒 under standard GB/T 18356, licence SC11552038220099, glutinous sorghum, wheat and water only, five years, and — in the label's own words — no added aromas or flavourings. It then describes its own bouquet as nine families of fragrance, flowers and fruit and dried botanicals and nuts among them. That is the most precise tasting note Moutai has ever published about itself.
