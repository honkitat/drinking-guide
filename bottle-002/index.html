BACKGROUND

The Carillon family has farmed in Puligny-Montrachet for roughly four centuries — “1611” is the date they print on the label as their founding, and the surname has been in the village longer than either Leflaive or Chartron. The domaine behind this bottle is much younger: François Carillon created it in 2010, when his father Louis retired and the family estate was divided amicably between François and his brother Jacques. Two domaines, one cellar history — which is why both Carillon labels carry the same weight in Puligny.

Les Folatières is the largest premier cru in Puligny, a steep east-to-south-east slope at about 276 m with thin soil and limestone scree showing through. Sitting high and catching the morning sun, it ripens early, and the wines usually come out broader and more generous than the tightest Puligny premiers crus — more stone fruit and honeysuckle than the razor edge of a Pucelles.

2023 was Burgundy's warmest year on record, and the whites came in generous: plenty of fruit and volume, with the risk being low acidity and higher alcohol where growers picked late. The best 2023 whites kept a racy line under all that richness, and this is a good address for it.
